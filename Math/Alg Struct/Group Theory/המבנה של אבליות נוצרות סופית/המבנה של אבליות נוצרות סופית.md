Compared to the sorting of general groups, the sorting of finite abelian group is very simple. Every finite abelian group is a direct product [[]] of cyclic groups. The same is true not only for finite abelian groups, but to the larger collection of "finitely generated" abelian groups.

>	Let $G$ be a group. $G$ is called "Finitely generated" when there are $g_1, g_2, \dots , g_k \in G$ for $k \in \mathbb{N}$ s.t. $G = \left < g_1, \dots , g_k \right >$

>Def (7.4) (Torsion):
>	>Let $G$ be a group. And $g \in G$. $g$ is called a "torsion" element of $G$ when the order of $g$ [[]] is finite (Meaning there is $r \in \mathbb{N}$ s.t. $g^r = e$).
>	
>	>$G$ is called a "torsion group" when all its element are torsion elements

>	>$G$ is called "torsion free" when all its elements (except $e$) are NOT torsion elements

^197b3a

For example, Every finite group is a trorsion group. A vector space [[]] of infinite dimention over a finite field [[]] is an infinite torsion group. And the groups $\mathbb{Z}$ and $\mathbb{Q}$ are torsion free.

>Def:
>	Let $A$ be an abelian group. We will denote $A_{tor}$ the set of all elements of $A$ that are torsion elements. Meaning
>		$$A_{tor}:= \left\{ a \in A \mid \exists m \in \mathbb{N}: ma = e_A \right\}$$

>Lemma (7.5):
>	Let $A$ be an abelian group. Then

>	>(1) $A_{tor} \leq A$ ^a23ec6

>	>(2) $A \big / A_{tor}$ is torsion free ^d94089

^f6142a

Note: About [[#^d94089 |Part (2) of the lemma]], the quotient is a group because any subgroup of $A$ is normal because $A$ is abelian.

>Proof:
>	>[[#^a23ec6 |(1):]]
>	>	$A_{tor}$ is closed for inverse ($m(-a) = -ma = -0 = 0$ Or in normal notation $\left(a^{-1}\right)^m = \left(a^m\right)^{-1} = e^{-1} = e$) and for sums (as $\left(a + b\right)^m = a^m + b^m$ since $A$ is abelian). And by [[]] (Exercise 1.63) we are done.
>	
>	>[[#^f6142a |(2):]]
>	>	Assume $a + A_{tor}$ in $A \big / A_{tor}$ is a torsion element, meaning that there is $m \in \mathbb{N}$ such that $ma + A_{tor} = m\left( a + A_{tor} \right) = A_{tor}$. Then $ma \in A_{tor}$, and thus there is some $n \in \mathbb{N}$ such that $n\left(ma\right) = 0$ thus $\left(nm\right)a = 0$ $\implies$ $a \in A_{tor}$ $\implies$ $a + A_{tor} = 0 + A_{tor} = A_{tor} = 0_{A \big / A_{tor}}$ So the only torsion element in the quotient is the $0$ element and [[#^197b3a |by definition]] the quotient is torsion free.
>	$\blacksquare$
